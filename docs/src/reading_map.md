# Choose a reading path

You can begin by watching a hole appear and disappear in a small image, or by
working through the definitions of a persistence module's spaces and maps.
Both routes lead to finite encodings, which let us describe those spaces and
maps using finite data.

The saved figures let you follow the notebook without installing Julia.

<!-- READING_MAP -->

If you begin with the example, “Why two parameters?”
will help you see why we need that description; you can then continue to
encodings or spend more time with the definitions first.

When you want to try the computations yourself, the installation guide explains
how to open it. The arrows suggest where a question developed in one lesson
can lead, so you can follow the route that fits what you want to understand.
They do not make the earlier lessons prerequisites for opening a later one.

After finite encodings, you can inspect the square example in code or see how
regions and a matrix describe its spaces and maps. Having a finite description
in hand makes it natural to ask when such descriptions exist, which is the
question developed in the lessons on tameness.

Lesson cards open pages on this site, including the ring and square inspection
notebooks. On a narrow screen, scroll sideways or use the linked outline below
the map. The focused summands diagram continues from the same inspected
finite module into an optional algebraic question.

The groups below the lessons collect guides for further questions about data,
algebra, figures and saving your work. Links that open repository documents are
labelled so you can tell where they lead.

To find different kinds of treatment of the same subject, use the
[topic map](topic_map.md). It groups documents by subject without prescribing
a reading order.
